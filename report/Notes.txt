2 Inspection of the service requests still open at the end of 2024
2.1 Visual inspection of the ticket age distribution suggests peaks at 60 and 170 days 
(@fig-open-tickets-distribution). Because it is a visual observation it could be an artefact of data binning 
and it is first confirmed by changing binning (@fig-robust-binning). To completely confirm 
the existence of the two peaks a robust nonparametric test (Hartigan's Dip Test) is utilized
and yields an effect size of 0.02 and a p-value indistiguishible from 0.
2.2 The origin of the two peaks is then resolved by cross-referencing open requests and departments.
The transportation department shows a surge of unclosed requests starting in June (170 days) followed
by a second one in Ocotber (60 days)(@@fig-open-tickets-by-month). These two surges correspond 
respectively to the beginning of the Sumner Tunnel Restoration Project and the temporary closure 
of the MBTA orange line.
3. Resolution time. The distribution of resolution times (closing - opening) is very skewed to 
the right, implying that the median resolution time is a better descriptor than the average. 
Moreover, even after log transformation, resolution times do not fit a normal distribution 
(@fig-resolution-density-log) implying that standard statistical approaches are inappropriate. 
Instead, robust nonparametric approaches are needed.
4. SLA compliance analytics. Focusing on closed requests for which there was a 
Service Level Agreement deadline, distribution shown in fig-sla-delta-distribution. 
4.1 Many requests are closed right before the deadline and a smaller set is lagging
(@fig-sla-delta-distribution).
4.2 To identify factors that determine early or late compliance, we use a robust nonparametric 
test (volume-weighted KS test). Results show that the main factors determining compliance are 
related to organizational routing ('type', 'reason', 'subject', 'department') (@tbl-factor-ranking).
While other factors yield smaller effect sizes, all have small p-values (likely related to the 
large sample size). So, it would be a statistical fallacy here to reject the presence of 
geographical inequalities, in particular because the utilized test is not designed to handle 
the concept of geographic proximity. Disparity of compliance for factor queue is illustrated 
with @fig-queue-histogram.
